# Ironwood Flake Monetary and Land-Reserve Policy

## Purpose

The **Ironwood Flake** is the replacement fiat currency for the current Ironwood run.

The Flake is not a commodity currency and is not a promise that every Flake can be redeemed for a fixed fraction of a specific parcel. Its monetary discipline comes from a **land-reserve issuance rule**: the issuing authority limits net currency in circulation according to the published face value of unsold civic land instruments it controls.

This policy is run-specific to Ironwood.

## Issuing authority

The Ironwood Treasury is the initial issuing authority for Flakes.

Flakes are intended to circulate beyond Ironwood. Players and future settlements may acquire Flakes through trade, public Exchange orders, public works, research procurement, and other authorized transactions with the issuing authority. Other Towns do not need to create separate currencies in order to participate in the Flake economy.

No non-authorized account may create new Flakes.

## Legacy FroBoyX Credit conversion

- Existing **FroBoyX Credit converts to Flakes at 1:1**.
- Conversion remains available permanently so absent or returning players do not lose the value of legacy balances.
- FroBoyX Credit is a legacy currency after cutover and is not newly issued.
- Credits surrendered through conversion must not remain simultaneously available for ordinary spending. Conversion must not duplicate purchasing power.

The purpose is replacement without confiscation, deadline pressure, or a haircut.

## Initial and future player capitalization

- Each player who is **Active at cutover** receives a one-time **180-Flake capitalization grant**.
- A player who is not Active at cutover receives the same **180-Flake grant once**, on the first occasion they become Active.
- The grant is per player, once only. Losing and regaining Active status does not create another grant.

The grant is startup liquidity, not payment for land. Players already possess their normal personal claim capacity under Eco's settlement system.

## Town real-estate schedule

Current Ironwood civic land prices are fixed as follows:

| Civic land instrument | Treasury Buy | Treasury Sell | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Town Claim Paper | 150 Flakes | 150 Flakes | Expands claim capacity on a Town property |
| Homestead Support Claim Paper | 100 Flakes | 100 Flakes | Restricted support claim; deliberately cheaper than a Town Claim |
| Town Claim Stake | 1,000 Flakes | 1,000 Flakes | Creates a new Town property and includes 5 claims |

The Treasury uses **no bid/ask spread** on these civic land instruments. Their purpose is to anchor land value and provide a predictable monetary sink/source, not to earn a trading margin.

### Claim Stake valuation

A Town Claim Stake includes five Town claims.

At the current Town Claim price:

- 5 included claims = 750 Flakes of embedded claim value;
- the remaining 250 Flakes is the premium for the scarce stake and the ability to create another independent Town property;
- total Town Claim Stake price = 1,000 Flakes.

Do not describe the 250-Flake stake/property premium as being equal to one 150-Flake claim. If the policy price remains 1,000, the premium is 250.

## Land reserve

### Reserve assets

The land reserve consists only of **unsold, sellable civic land instruments actually controlled by the issuing authority** at their current published Treasury prices.

For the current Town this includes, where held by the Treasury/Foundation:

- Town Claim Papers;
- Homestead Support Claim Papers;
- Town Claim Stakes.

Assets already distributed to players are not counted as Treasury reserve merely because the settlement originally generated them.

### Issuance ceiling

The maximum **net Flake circulation outside the issuer's sterilized reserve balances** is:

> **60% × current face value of the issuer-controlled sellable land reserve**

This is the **printable reserve ceiling**.

The 60% factor deliberately leaves a 40% buffer against changes in land inventory, repurchases, population, settlement reorganization, and pricing mistakes.

The ceiling is a maximum, not an issuance target. The Treasury does not print to the ceiling merely because capacity exists.

### Current observed reserve snapshot

At the 2026-08-15 Foundation snapshot, Ironwood visibly held:

- 167 Town Claim Papers;
- 140 Homestead Support Claim Papers;
- 35 Town Claim Stakes.

At current prices this is a face-value reserve of:

- Town Claims: 167 × 150 = 25,050 Flakes;
- Homestead Support Claims: 140 × 100 = 14,000 Flakes;
- Town Claim Stakes: 35 × 1,000 = 35,000 Flakes;
- total face value = **74,050 Flakes**.

The corresponding 60% printable reserve ceiling is **44,430 Flakes**.

This is a snapshot, not a permanent number. Reserve value must be recalculated as inventory or published land prices change.

## Land sales, repurchases, and monetary treatment

Land transactions must preserve the reserve rule.

### When the Treasury sells a claim or stake

- the civic land asset leaves issuer inventory;
- reserve face value falls by the published price of that asset;
- the Flakes paid back to the Treasury are **sterilized from ordinary spending** or retired where mechanics permit;
- those receipts are not treated as ordinary spendable revenue while needed to maintain the reserve rule.

This prevents the Treasury from simultaneously losing the backing asset and immediately re-spending the money received for it.

### When the Treasury buys back a claim or stake

- the civic land asset returns to issuer inventory;
- reserve face value rises by its published price;
- the Treasury returns the same fixed amount of Flakes to the seller;
- a repurchase may occur only when doing so remains inside the 60% issuance ceiling.

The equal buy/sell price provides predictable convertibility, but repurchases are necessarily subject to Treasury solvency/reserve capacity and actual transferability of the land instrument.

## Relationship to ordinary economic activity

The land reserve controls how much currency may be issued; it does **not** replace the productive economy.

Flakes enter player hands primarily through useful activity, including:

- government and Exchange purchases of goods;
- public works;
- research procurement;
- authorized civic contracts;
- inter-settlement trade and procurement;
- the one-time player capitalization grant;
- legacy FroBoyX Credit conversion during the transition.

Claims and stakes provide a durable way for economically successful players to return currency to the issuing authority in exchange for scarce expansion rights.

The intended monetary cycle is:

> **issue for useful activity → circulate through trade → return through land/civic purchases → sterilize or retire → reissue only when reserve and economic need justify it**

## Future Country

Formation of a Country creates an additional settlement layer with its own generated claims and stakes. Those Country assets are **additive** to the land resources already generated by member Towns.

Country claims/stakes are also more flexible because they can be used across the Country's member Town structure. They must therefore be treated as a strategic national reserve rather than cheap replacement inventory for Town claims.

When the Country is founded:

1. Country-controlled sellable claim/stake inventory is counted only after it actually exists and is under the issuing authority's control;
2. separate Country land prices must be published before those assets receive reserve value;
3. 60% of that additional face value may increase the printable reserve ceiling;
4. the Country must not price its cross-Town claims so cheaply that it destroys member-Town land policy or makes Town claims economically irrelevant;
5. Country inventory need not be placed entirely into unrestricted retail sale merely because it contributes to reserve capacity.

The creation of additional reserve capacity permits additional currency issuance, but does not require it.

## Policy principles

1. **No confiscatory currency reset.** Legacy Credit keeps 1:1 value into Flakes.
2. **No arbitrary money printing.** Net circulation is constrained by a published reserve rule.
3. **No cheap civic-land liquidation.** Claims and stakes are finite settlement assets, not ordinary commodities.
4. **No double counting.** Distributed land assets and surrendered legacy currency cannot simultaneously remain on both sides of the system.
5. **No automatic inflation to the ceiling.** Monetary capacity is not a spending mandate.
6. **Productive participation remains the normal way to earn currency.**
7. **The reserve expands with legitimate new settlement-controlled land capacity**, including a future Country, only after that capacity exists and has a published valuation.
