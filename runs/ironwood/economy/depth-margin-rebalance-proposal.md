# Ironwood Depth-Margin Rebalance Proposal

This proposal applies the depth-sensitive commercial-viability policy to the currently rebuilt Carpentry, Basic Engineering and Mechanics tables.

It is a **price proposal for review**. Raw/gathered anchors are unchanged. Bulk construction prices (Brick, Glass, Lumber) remain on their separate construction-band policy.

## Depth ladder used

- Depth 0 gathered/raw: anchor-priced; no manufactured-goods multiplier.
- Depth 1 simple processing/components: about 10–12% over Exchange-sourced cash cost.
- Depth 2 ordinary finished goods/multi-step intermediates: about 15–18%.
- Depth 3 deep manufactured goods: about 25%.
- Capital goods/workstations/vehicles: about 30%.
- Bulk construction: separate structural valuation.

The cost basis below is the entry recipe using **Town Sell prices for purchased inputs**, plus labor and known disposal costs. This is deliberately stricter than the structural producer-cost calculation.

## Commercial surplus examples

The proposed Town Buy values leave the following entry-level cash surplus even if all priced inputs are bought from the Exchange:

- Machinist Table: about **11.52 credits** per unit;
- Arrastra: about **2.22**;
- Basic Engineering Upgrade: about **6.29**;
- Boiler: about **7.99**;
- Lathe: about **13.07**;
- Stamp Mill: about **7.31**;
- Steam Truck Flatbed: about **7.07**;
- Screening Machine: about **20.03**;
- Mechanics Advanced Upgrade: about **15.95**.

Those are the amounts that can actually fund food, input-store buy orders, inventory and workshop expansion. Higher skill and better infrastructure increase them further.

## What does not change

This proposal does **not** apply the deep-goods uplift to gathered anchors such as Wood, Sand, Clay, Rock, Limestone or raw ores. It also does not automatically raise bulk construction prices beyond the already accepted Brick/Glass/Lumber band.
