# Pricing Doctrine

## Purpose

Define how canonical prices are set across the Eco 14 planned economy.

The economy is intentionally managed. Prices are not expected to float freely until equilibrium emerges; instead, the public economy establishes anchors and reference prices that make profession participation, progression, and downstream costing predictable.

## Primary Rule

**Canonical sale prices should usually remain stable as a profession becomes more efficient.**

A beginning producer should have a shallow but positive margin at reasonable input prices. As the producer gains skill levels, modules, improved crafting stations, infrastructure, logistics, or specialization, their effective cost per unit falls and their profit margin grows.

This means progression creates economic reward without requiring automatic price inflation.

## Price Construction

For each good, determine:

1. **Input cost** — quantity of each consumed input multiplied by its canonical value.
2. **Expected waste/byproduct value** — subtract reusable or independently valuable outputs where appropriate.
3. **Processing burden** — workstation access, fuel, power, transport, time, and complexity.
4. **Entry-level margin** — a deliberately shallow profit allowance for a competent but inefficient producer.
5. **Strategic adjustment** — only when necessary to preserve participation, progression, public works, environmental behavior, or scarcity management.

The result becomes a reference sale price or an explicit government buy/sell price.

## Margin Philosophy

The system does not target the same percentage margin for every item. It targets comparable **economic opportunity**.

Low-level staple production should normally be viable without producing runaway wealth. High-level efficiency should make established producers meaningfully more profitable because they use less labor, fewer resources, less fuel, less power, or less waste for the same output.

Where a recipe is naturally inefficient at low skill, the canonical price must be high enough that the profession is still worth playing, but should not price in the best possible efficiency as though every producer already has it.

## Government Market Role

Government shops may be used to:

- establish raw-material anchors;
- provide an import ceiling when a shortage would stall progression;
- provide a purchase floor where a profession otherwise has no reliable customer;
- buy public goods and infrastructure;
- pay for cleanup, waste collection, or environmental services;
- stabilize strategic materials;
- distribute currency through useful work rather than arbitrary grants.

Government shops should avoid replacing a player profession when players can reasonably provide the same function.

## Import and Export Meaning

For this project:

- **Government buys from players** when the economy needs a reliable demand floor.
- **Government sells/imports to players** when the economy needs a reliable supply ceiling.
- **Negative-valued waste intake** means the government pays the player to surrender the item because accepting/disposal of that item is itself a public service.

Spread between government buy and sell prices should generally be modest but sufficient to discourage trivial arbitrage.

## Price Propagation

When a raw-material anchor changes, all dependent products must be reviewed.

A downstream price should not survive merely because it was previously convenient. Changes to logs, stone, ores, bars, food staples, fuels, fibers, or recycled inputs can alter several professions at once.

Eventually the repository should hold structured recipe and price data so these effects can be audited automatically.

## Participation Test

Before locking a price, ask:

- Can a low-efficiency producer make a small profit?
- Does greater efficiency improve that producer's margin?
- Does the price create useful demand for upstream professions?
- Does a government shop accidentally bypass a player profession?
- Does the price create an obvious arbitrage loop?
- Does it distort progression by making research, tools, food, housing, or transport impossibly expensive or effectively free?
- Does it reward environmentally damaging behavior more strongly than cleaner alternatives without a deliberate reason?

## Status Labels

All numeric values should use one of these labels until the economy is fully normalized:

- **LOCKED** — explicitly accepted and current.
- **DERIVED** — calculated from locked inputs and current doctrine.
- **PROVISIONAL** — currently usable but still under review.
- **RECOVERED** — taken from prior chat work and awaiting confirmation or cross-check.
- **SUPERSEDED** — retained only for history and not to be used.

No unlabeled recovered number should be treated as canonical.
