# Governance Model

## Purpose

Define the intended civic architecture for the Eco 14 world and the policy tests used when creating laws.

The government exists to steer behavior and maintain a functioning shared economy, not merely to collect taxes.

## Hierarchy

### Town

The Town is the first operational government and should handle matters that are strongly local:

- roads, sidewalks, and public works;
- local land-use restrictions and districts;
- local harvesting and cleanup rules;
- municipal waste collection;
- town market and exchange policy;
- local subsidies, reimbursements, and public-service payments;
- local environmental protections;
- access rules for shared civic resources.

The Town should be capable of solving practical local problems without waiting for a higher government.

### Country

The Country coordinates systems that cross Town boundaries or benefit from consistent standards:

- national currency and broad fiscal policy;
- major infrastructure;
- resource extraction standards;
- pollution controls;
- research and progression policy;
- inter-town trade and strategic reserves;
- national waste and recycling standards;
- common legal rights and enforcement rules.

Country law should avoid taking over local functions unless uniformity or cross-border effects justify it.

### Federation

The Federation coordinates multiple constituent governments while preserving useful local and national authority.

Likely Federation concerns include:

- shared environmental minimums;
- cross-border pollution and resource rules;
- intergovernmental trade standards;
- common infrastructure expectations;
- federation-wide strategic goals;
- mechanisms for cooperation between constituent governments.

The Federation should not exist merely to duplicate lower-level law.

## Three Co-Equal Policy Goals

Every significant law should be evaluated against three goals:

1. **Minimize pollution and destructive extraction.**
2. **Maximize meaningful participation by all professions.**
3. **Keep economic and technological progression moving steadily.**

A law that achieves one goal by badly damaging the other two should be treated as suspect.

## Mechanical Reality Rule

A law is only operational if Eco 14 can actually express and enforce it.

For every proposed law, document:

- triggering **EVENT**;
- applicable **CONDITIONS**;
- resulting **ACTION**;
- targeted demographic, district, account, title, or jurisdiction where relevant;
- expected behavior when the actor cannot pay or otherwise fails a condition.

Natural-language intent is not enough. If the game cannot execute the intended rule, the law must be redesigned around mechanics that exist.

## Legal Design Principles

### Prefer incentives where possible

Use taxes, subsidies, refunds, public purchase programs, and public works payments when they can produce the desired behavior without unnecessary prohibition.

### Use prohibitions where incentives cannot protect a shared resource

Some behavior may need a hard gate when the environmental or public cost is too high and post-action taxation cannot undo the damage.

### Do not create unfunded mandates

If government requires cleanup, infrastructure, disposal, or other costly behavior, verify that the economy gives players a viable means to perform it.

### Do not strand professions

Before banning or heavily taxing an activity, identify which profession depends on it and whether an alternative economic path remains.

### Align public spending with useful work

Government-created currency should preferably enter circulation through public works, research, cleanup, environmental services, infrastructure, or strategic production rather than unconditional payouts.

### Audit non-citizen behavior

Any law intended to affect residents, visitors, or non-citizens must be tested against Eco 14's actual account and demographic behavior. Do not assume a tax or account action that works for citizens will work identically for all actors.

## Law Record Template

Each canonical law should eventually record:

- Name
- Government level
- Policy objective
- Jurisdiction/district
- Event
- Conditions
- Actions
- Payment source/destination
- Failure behavior
- Intended profession effects
- Pollution/resource effects
- Known exploit or edge-case risks
- Verification status in Eco 14

## Evolution Rule

Town laws are not automatically copied upward when the Country or Federation forms.

At each stage, review whether the matter belongs at the new level, should remain local, should be standardized, or should be retired because later technology/economic institutions make it unnecessary.
